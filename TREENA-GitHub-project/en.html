Tutor Order Explorer

A teacher-facing tutoring opportunity browser that turns structured order information into a searchable, filterable interface. Teachers can narrow opportunities by location, subject, teacher type, schedule, and hourly rate, then copy a complete order summary for follow-up.

  • Combined filters, order pools, pagination, and an empty-results state.
  • Responsive layouts and a mobile filter panel.
  • Clipboard copy with a manual fallback; area and subject summaries.
  • Separate public service guides for parents and teachers, with canonical metadata, structured data, and a sitemap.
Why it matters for an Agent role

The structured order fields provide a concrete basis for discussing natural-language intent extraction, tool parameters, and explainable matching.

Possible next step

Add an assistant that translates a teacher’s preferences into filters and explains each match. Order availability and final coordination would still require confirmation.

Scope: The reviewed artifact is a browser-based demo. It does not establish a deployed matching agent or production backend.

TrademarkFlow

A trademark case management application documented as a Next.js implementation with separate domain logic and storage adapters. It brings case stages, progress, risk, priority, ownership, and next actions into a single operational view.

  • An overview dashboard with seven dynamic KPIs and P0/P1 priorities.
  • Searchable case lists and detail pages with stage duration, remaining days, risk reasons, payment, and certificate status.
  • Domain-level calculations for progress, risk, and priority, consumed by the UI and API.
  • Local JSON persistence with eight idempotently seeded demo cases; a repository interface and a Prisma/PostgreSQL adapter for a later storage switch.
Why it matters for an Agent role

Explicit business rules and a repository boundary make useful foundations for controlled tool access and traceable recommendations.

Possible next step

Add a read-only case assistant that retrieves current records, explains priority, and proposes next actions for a human to approve.

Evidence: Features and architecture are described in the repository README. Its typecheck, lint, test, and build commands were not executed during this portfolio review.

PRIVATE · ACCESS REQUIREDREUSABLE AI SKILLINSTRUCTION DESIGN

Editorial Skill Library

The gongzhonghao-SKILL repository currently contains a reviewed literary-philosopher skill: a reusable instruction package for reflective, reference-rich writing. It specifies when the skill should activate, how to build an argument, and how to check the result.

  • Explicit triggers and exclusions for task routing.
  • A staged writing workflow: identify the underlying question, state a position, choose references, develop the argument, and close thoughtfully.
  • Style and length guidance, plus adjustments for different contexts.
  • Rules against invented references and excessive quotation, followed by a self-review checklist.
Why it matters for an Agent role

This is a concrete example of converting a writing preference into a reusable behavioral specification with activation rules and quality constraints.

Possible next step

Build a small evaluation set to test activation precision, factual references, style adherence, and behavior on excluded tasks.

Scope: The reviewed file is a skill specification. It does not establish an automated WeChat publishing integration.

02 / About me

“I start with business problems and turn them into AI workflows.”

Hello, my name is Wang Hui. I graduated from Southwest Minzu University with a degree in Economics. My experience combines business analysis, process improvement, and practical AI applications. I would like to highlight three experiences.

First, I have been developing my own AI agent practices and business tools through Tutor Planet. I started with fragmented orders, inefficient manual searches, duplicate records, and unclear ownership across partner organizations. I mapped the workflow, wrote the PRD, and used AI tools to build a website and an internal system connecting order entry, filtering, follow-up, and settlement. Along the way, I developed a personal skill library, turning recurring requirements, execution steps, and quality checks into reusable instructions and refining them through prototype testing. This taught me to define the task clearly before deciding what information, steps, and checks an agent needs.

Second, at Feilong Consulting, I led the development of a digital trademark tracking system. My work in client needs analysis and delivery coordination involved tracking multiple process stages, identifying exceptions, and coordinating next steps. I made the workflow, milestones, and exceptions visible, and connected tracking with knowledge capture and intelligent responses. This experience showed me why clear processes and maintainable information matter when applying AI to business operations.

Third, at Sanweijia, I worked on AI product applications and solutions around AI Dream Home and AI Light Design. I investigated home-furnishing companies’ needs in marketing conversion, design efficiency, and customer communication, and participated in product demonstrations, application testing, and fit assessment. I also designed a multi-agent collaboration approach for five tasks: organizing customer information, screening leads, analyzing needs, planning communication, and managing follow-ups. I used prompts, skills, and workflows to structure execution and create reusable templates.

The strength I would bring to an AI Agent role is the ability to understand a business problem, break it into concrete tasks, and turn the solution into a workflow that can be tested and improved.
